# StorySpace — primary and review literature

Curated starting references. Links are cited to support **limited claims**, not to imply that the author(s) endorse EFMW, Monolithic, or StorySpace.

## Familiarity, memory and déjà vu

1. Cleary, Anne M. (2008). **Recognition Memory, Familiarity, and Déjà vu Experiences.** *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, 17(5). DOI: [10.1111/j.1467-8721.2008.00605.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8721.2008.00605.x). Reviews recognition without identification as one possible explanation for déjà vu.
2. Cleary, Anne M.; Ryals, Anthony J.; Nomi, Jason S. (2009). **Can déjà vu result from similarity to a prior experience? Support for the similarity hypothesis of déjà vu.** *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 16, 1082–1088. DOI: [10.3758/PBR.16.6.1082](https://doi.org/10.3758/PBR.16.6.1082). In a laboratory task, similarity to previously viewed scenes increased familiarity and déjà vu reports without recollection.
3. Cleary, Anne M.; Claxton, Alexander B. (2018). **Déjà Vu: An Illusion of Prediction.** *Psychological Science*, 29(4). DOI: [10.1177/0956797617743018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617743018). Reports greater *feelings* of being able to predict a navigation turn during induced déjà vu, without above-chance next-turn prediction in that task; study materials and an experimental preregistration are linked in the publication.
4. Martin, Chris B.; Fiacconi, Chris M.; Köhler, Stefan (2015). **Déjà Vu: A Window into Understanding the Cognitive Neuroscience of Familiarity.** In *The Wiley Handbook on the Cognitive Neuroscience of Memory*. DOI: [10.1002/9781118332634.ch9](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118332634.ch9). Reviews familiarity/novelty conflicts and relevant clinical literature.

## Quantum post-selection and interpretation

5. Scully, Marlan O.; Drühl, Kai (1982). **Quantum eraser: A proposed photon correlation experiment concerning observation and 'delayed choice' in quantum mechanics.** *Physical Review A* 25, 2208. DOI: [10.1103/PhysRevA.25.2208](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.25.2208).
6. Kim, Y.-H.; Yu, R.; Kulik, S. P.; Shih, Y. H.; Scully, M. O. (2000). **Delayed 'Choice' Quantum Eraser.** *Physical Review Letters* 84, 1–5. DOI: [10.1103/PhysRevLett.84.1](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.84.1). A landmark photon-correlation experiment. The conventional operational analysis does not require changing an already recorded unconditioned earlier distribution.
7. Cramer, John G. (1986). **The Transactional Interpretation of Quantum Mechanics.** *Reviews of Modern Physics* 58, 647. DOI: [10.1103/RevModPhys.58.647](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.58.647). Foundational interpretive proposal—not independent experimental evidence of backwards signaling.
8. Aharonov, Yakir; Bergmann, Peter G.; Lebowitz, Joel L. (1964). **Time Symmetry in the Quantum Process of Measurement.** *Physical Review* 134, B1410. DOI: [10.1103/PhysRev.134.B1410](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.134.B1410). A formal framework employing time-symmetric pre/post-selection.

## Methodology

9. Brier, Glenn W. (1950). **Verification of Forecasts Expressed in Terms of Probability.** *Monthly Weather Review* 78(1), 1–3. DOI: [10.1175/1520-0493(1950)078<0001:VOFEIT>2.0.CO;2](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(1950)078%3C0001:VOFEIT%3E2.0.CO;2). Basis for proper binary probability scoring.
10. [Open Science Framework — Preregistration](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/prereg). General methodological background on precommitting analysis plans.

### Selection policy

Prioritize primary research and discipline-specific reviews. New citations require a rationale explaining what claim they support and the limits of their applicability. None of these studies establishes a human retrocausal sensing mechanism.
